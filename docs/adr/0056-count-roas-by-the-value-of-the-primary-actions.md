# Count ROAS by the value of the primary conversion actions

## Context and Problem Statement

ROAS is a KPI of the three marts of statistics, `ad_stats_campaign_daily`, `ad_stats_ad_group_daily` and `ad_stats_ad_daily`, declared once in `dbt/models/marts/_marts__models.yml` under the anchor `&roas`: the sum of `conversion_value_reporting` divided by the sum of `spend_reporting`. `metabase/metrics.py` keeps it as the metrics `ROAS (Campaigns)`, `ROAS (Ad Groups)` and `ROAS (Ads)` of Metabase and as a term of its glossary ([ADR-0034](0034-define-a-basic-data-set-and-the-modules-of-an-extended-one.md)). `conversion_value` is the value of the conversions of the own metric of conversions of the platform on the row of statistics (the doc block `conversion_value` of `dbt/models/_docs.md`), as [ADR-0037](0037-serve-the-marts-as-views-over-the-core.md) keeps `conversions` the metric that the account shows. Read in the code of `main` at `798687b` on 26 November 2026, the platforms fill it like this:

* **Google Ads** gives it: `metrics.conversions_value` on each row of campaigns, ad groups and ads (`google_ads_stats` of `dbt/macros/shapes/google_ads_v25.sql`), the value of the actions that Google counts in `metrics.conversions`.
* **Meta** has no metric of conversions of its own and counts each action type apart, so `meta_stats` of `dbt/macros/shapes/meta_v26.sql` gives no value at any level.
* **TikTok** has an own metric, `conversion`, the event that a campaign optimizes for, but no value for it, so `tiktok_stats` of `dbt/macros/shapes/tiktok_v1_3.sql` gives no value either.

Meta and TikTok do give a value by conversion action. `meta_conversions` reads `value` of each action type in `action_values`, and `tiktok_conversions` reads the value of the complete payments, `total_complete_payment_rate`. Both reach `core.ad_conversions_campaign_daily`, one row for each campaign, day and action, as the value of Google by `segments.conversion_action` does (`google_ads_conversions`, and `fact_conversions_rows` of `dbt/macros/facts.sql`). `conversions_primary` of `ad_stats_campaign_daily` already adds up the conversions of the primary actions of the deployment (`is_primary`) from that fact, through `all_conversions` where the platform has it, in a `LEFT JOIN LATERAL` of the view (`dbt/models/marts/ad_stats_campaign_daily.sql`), and the cards Conversions and Cost per conversion of the dashboard Advertising count it. No column adds up the value of those actions.

That gives three faults:

* The ROAS of Meta and of TikTok is empty at every level.
* A ROAS over several platforms is too low: their spend goes into the denominator, and none of their value into the numerator.
* The cards of the Overview mix two bases: Conversions and Cost per conversion count the primary actions of the deployment, and ROAS, beside them, the own metric of each platform.

A local stack with the demo, read on 26 November 2026 over the default period of the dashboard, the 12 weeks from 31 August to 23 November 2026, shows the second fault. Its ROAS over all platforms is 3.20, with 5.26 on `demo_search` and none on `demo_social` and `demo_video`. The same rows, with the value of the primary actions as the numerator, give 4.88 in all: 5.26 on `demo_search`, 4.45 on `demo_social` and 3.69 on `demo_video`.

How does agoranomos count ROAS, so that it has a value on every platform that reports one, and a total over platforms says what it adds up?

## Decision Drivers

* A KPI has a value on every platform that reports its inputs, Meta and TikTok among them.
* The numerator and the denominator of a ratio cover the same rows: no spend in the denominator without the value that the platform reports for it.
* A card of ROAS counts the same actions as the cards of the conversions beside it.
* The own metric of each platform stays, so that a person can compare a number with the account (ADR-0037).
* The description of a metric says what it counts, and that a sum over platforms can count one purchase more than once.
* No new request to a platform, no migration of the core, and no second lookup for each row of the view.

## Considered Options

For the value of ROAS:

* The value of the primary actions, `conversion_value_primary`, added up as `conversions_primary` is
* The own metric of conversions of each platform, as today, with Meta and TikTok empty and a note
* The own ROAS of each platform only, with no ROAS over platforms
* The ROAS that each platform computes, such as `purchase_roas` of Meta and `complete_payment_roas` of TikTok
* One source of revenue, such as GA4 or a CRM, over the spend of the platforms

For the names in Metabase, with the value of the primary actions:

* `Primary ROAS` for the primary actions beside `ROAS` for the own metric, as `Cost per Primary Conversion` stands beside `Cost per Conversion`
* `ROAS` for the primary actions, and `Platform ROAS` for the own metric

## Decision Outcome

Recommended, for the maintainer to accept or change: "The value of the primary actions" and "`Primary ROAS` for the primary actions beside `ROAS` for the own metric". The first gives every platform a ROAS from what it reports by action, over the same actions as the conversions of the dashboard. The second follows the pair of conversions that the marts already have, so no metric of today changes its meaning, and the dashboard shows the new one wherever it shows the conversions of the primary actions.

In practice this means:

* **The columns.** `ad_stats_campaign_daily` gets `conversion_value_primary`, the value of the primary actions of the campaign on the day in the currency of the account, and `conversion_value_primary_reporting`, the same at the rate of its day, as `conversion_value_reporting` is computed. The value is the sum of `coalesce(all_conversion_value, conversion_value)` over the rows of `core.ad_conversions_campaign_daily` whose action is primary, as `conversions_primary` sums `coalesce(all_conversions, conversions)`. The `LEFT JOIN LATERAL` that gives `conversions_primary` returns both sums, so a question that reads both columns still makes one lookup in the key of the fact for each row. The value is 0 on a whole day of a campaign without a primary conversion, and none on a partial day (`is_partial_day`), as `conversions_primary` is. `ad_stats_ad_group_daily` and `ad_stats_ad_daily` get both columns as none, as they have `conversions_primary`, until the module of the conversions by action of those levels ([ADR-0036](0036-model-the-core-as-narrow-facts-and-versioned-dimensions.md)), planned for v0.9. The columns go after those of today, as ADR-0037 adds new columns after those of v0.4, and no table of the core changes.
* **The KPI.** A KPI `Primary ROAS` of the campaigns, the sum of `conversion_value_primary_reporting` divided by the sum of `spend_reporting`, in the format `number`, which `metabase-init` keeps as the metric `Primary ROAS (Campaigns)` and a term of the glossary, as it keeps `Cost per Primary Conversion`. Its description says that the primary actions are the choice of the deployment, that it has a value on every platform that reports a value for them, and that each platform attributes conversions by its own model and windows, so a ROAS over platforms can count the value of one purchase more than once.
* **ROAS stays the own metric.** `ROAS` keeps its numerator and denominator at the three levels. Its description adds that it has no value on Meta and TikTok, and that over several platforms it divides the value of those that report one by the spend of all.
* **The dashboard Advertising** (`metabase/advertising.py`). The trend ROAS of the Overview, the tables Platforms and Top campaigns by spend, and the table of the tab Campaigns take `Primary ROAS`, under the title ROAS, as the card Cost per conversion takes `Cost per Primary Conversion` today. Results by platform shows both, with `conversion_value_primary_reporting` beside `conversion_value_reporting`. The tab Ad groups and ads keeps `ROAS`, the only one of its levels, as it keeps the own metric of conversions. The text About these numbers says that the value of the conversions, and so ROAS, is that of the primary actions. The demo dashboard of `metabase/demo_init.py` takes the value of the primary actions too.
* **The metric definitions of v0.8.** `marts.metric_definitions` of [ADR-0043](0043-define-the-metrics-in-the-warehouse-and-give-agents-tools-over-the-marts.md) gets one row more, `Primary ROAS` of the campaigns, from the same YAML, and `stats_by` offers it as it offers any other metric. Nothing of that design changes.
* **Google Ads.** The two values should agree where the deployment keeps the primary actions of Google, but for the three cases below. Today's `conversion_value` of a campaign is `metrics.conversions_value` of its row, which in the reference of v26 "only includes conversion actions which include_in_conversions_metric attribute is set to true". `conversion_value_primary` adds up `metrics.all_conversions_value` by `segments.conversion_action`, "The value of all conversions", over the actions that the deployment holds primary. Without an entry in `extensions/model/conversion_actions.toml`, those are the actions whose `include_in_conversions_metric` the newest list of conversion actions of the account sets (`int_conversion_actions__resolved`). The guide of conversion goals says that for an action with `primary_for_goal` "Google Ads includes the conversion action's conversions and conversion values in Conversions and All conversions", so by action the value of a primary action should be the same in both metrics, which the reference does not state (More Information). The two differ:
    * for a campaign with a custom conversion goal: Google does not count a secondary action in Conversions "unless you use the conversion action in a CustomConversionGoal", so such a campaign counts the value of a secondary action of its goal, which `conversion_value_primary` leaves out;
    * where `include_in_conversions_metric`, which the guide calls deprecated, and `primary_for_goal` disagree on an action;
    * where the flag of an action changed: the newest list gives it for every day, as for `conversions_primary` today.

    When the deployment changes the primary actions, `Primary ROAS` follows its choice. It counts the value of an action that it makes primary, which `metrics.conversions_value` leaves out, and leaves out one that it makes secondary, so it differs from the ROAS of the account, as `Cost per Primary Conversion` differs from `Cost per Conversion`. The demo platform `demo_search` gives the same value in both metrics for each primary action, and its ROAS is 5.26 on both bases above.
* **Attribution.** Each platform credits a conversion to its own ads by its own model and windows. Google does it by the attribution model and the lookback windows of the conversion action, Meta by the attribution setting of the ad set (ADR-0050), and TikTok by the windows of the ad group, up to 28 days after a click and 7 after a view. A purchase that followed a click on Google and a view on Meta can count on both. That is true of every sum of the figures of the platforms, `conversions_primary` among them, and the descriptions of `Primary ROAS`, of `conversion_value_primary` and the text About these numbers say so. A ROAS without the double count needs one source of revenue, which stays out of this record (below).

The other options were not chosen for these reasons:

* **The own metric, as today, with a note** keeps the ROAS of Meta and TikTok empty and the total too low, whatever the note says, and keeps the Overview on two bases. A person who reads the total ROAS and no note gets a number that leaves out the value of Meta and TikTok and keeps their spend.
* **The own ROAS of each platform only** removes the faulty total from the Overview, but a metric of Metabase cannot keep a question from adding up the rows of several platforms, so `ROAS (Campaigns)` over several platforms stays as low as today, and Meta and TikTok stay empty. It changes only the cards: the trend ROAS of the Overview goes, and the tables by platform and by campaign keep it.
* **The ROAS that each platform computes** is a ratio on each row, which the marts do not store, as a sum of ratios is wrong (ADR-0037). Google has no such field, and the core already holds the value of the purchases of Meta and TikTok by action.
* **One source of revenue** counts each purchase once, by its own attribution, but needs a match of its sessions or orders to the campaigns of the platforms. `web_sessions_daily` has the revenue of the sessions by source, medium and campaign, and names the platform and the account of the ad campaign whose ID the links carry, `ad_platform` and `ad_account_id` ([ADR-0054](0054-connect-ga4-with-a-service-account-of-the-deployment.md)). A ROAS of that revenue over the spend of those campaigns would count only the sessions whose links carry the ID of a campaign, and credit each by the attribution of web analytics; no KPI or card joins the two yet, and a CRM is no source of agoranomos. It is a follow-up beside this record, not instead of it: a ROAS by the revenue of web analytics, as a KPI of its own.
* **`ROAS` for the primary actions and `Platform ROAS`** would change the meaning of the metrics `ROAS (Campaigns)`, `ROAS (Ad Groups)` and `ROAS (Ads)` that exist today. `ROAS (Ad Groups)` and `ROAS (Ads)` would go, as their levels have no value of the primary actions, and `metabase-init` puts an item that the code no longer ships into the trash (`metabase/content.py`). The KPI `ROAS` would then exist at the campaigns alone, and the own metric would take a name that the conversions do not use. A name such as `ROAS (platform)` does not fit the rule `<KPI> (<level>)` of [Describe the marts](../reference/describe-the-marts.md#metrics), as it would become `ROAS (platform) (Campaigns)`.

### Consequences

* Good, because Meta and TikTok get a ROAS from the value that they already report by action, with no new request and no migration of the core.
* Good, because a ROAS over platforms divides the value of every platform by the spend of every platform, and the Overview counts its conversions, its cost per conversion and its ROAS over the same actions.
* Good, because the metrics of today keep their meaning, and `ROAS` still matches the ROAS of an account of Google.
* Bad, because two ROAS sit side by side in Metabase, the glossary and `metric_definitions`, and each needs its description to tell them apart, as the two costs per conversion do today.
* Bad, because a total over platforms now counts more of the double attribution, as it adds up the value of Meta and TikTok too. The descriptions say it, and a ROAS without it waits for one source of revenue.
* Bad, because the value follows the choice of the deployment: two primary actions that count one purchase count its value twice, as Meta's `offsite_conversion.fb_pixel_purchase` and `omni_purchase` would, which `seed_conversion_actions` keeps apart by making only the first primary.
* Bad, because `Primary ROAS` has no value at the levels of ad groups and ads until the module of their conversions by action, and those levels keep the own metric, empty on Meta and TikTok.
* Bad, because the ROAS of the Overview of a deployment of Google can differ from the account where a campaign has a custom conversion goal or where the deployment changed the primary actions, as the Conversions card already can.
* Neutral, because a campaign day that Google gives only in the report of conversions has no row of statistics in staging, so its value is missing from `conversion_value_primary`, as its conversions are from `conversions_primary` today; `assert_primary_actions_add_up_to_the_conversions` warns of it.
* Neutral, because the view of campaigns adds one sum to the lookup that it already makes, which the profile of an agency measures before the change ships (Confirmation).

### Confirmation

The singular test `assert_primary_actions_add_up_to_the_conversions` compares `conversion_value_primary` too with the sum of the values of the primary actions in `ad_conversions_campaign_daily`. Unit tests check the KPI `Primary ROAS`, its numerator and denominator, and its place on the campaigns alone (`tests/unit/test_metrics.py`, `test_dbt_project.py`), and the cards of the dashboard and of the demo that take it (`test_advertising.py`, `test_demo_init.py`). Stack tests check that `Primary ROAS (Campaigns)` has a value on `demo_social` and `demo_video`, that it equals `ROAS (Campaigns)` on `demo_search`, and that the columns are none on a partial day (`tests/stack/test_metrics.py`, `test_meta_ads.py`, `test_tiktok_ads.py`, `test_partial_day.py`). The measurement of the profile of an agency (`tests/stack/test_profile.py`) checks that the cards of the Overview stay within the threshold of ADR-0037. On the long-running stand, which loads a real account of Google Ads, a query compares `conversion_value_primary` with `conversion_value` by campaign and day for the accounts that keep the primary actions of Google, and names each campaign where they differ.

* **The facts behind this record**, each read on 26 November 2026:
    * The field reference of the Google Ads API v26 ([metrics](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/fields/v26/metrics), [segments](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/fields/v26/segments), [conversion_action](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/fields/v26/conversion_action)). `metrics.conversions` is "The number of conversions. This only includes conversion actions which include_in_conversions_metric attribute is set to true", and `metrics.conversions_value` "The value of conversions", with the same condition. `metrics.all_conversions` "includes all conversions regardless of the value of include_in_conversions_metric", and `metrics.all_conversions_value` is "The value of all conversions". `segments.conversion_action`, the "Resource name of the conversion action", can be selected with the four of them and not with `metrics.cost_micros`, `metrics.clicks` or `metrics.impressions`, so the value by action comes in a report of its own, as ADR-0034 has it. `conversion_action.include_in_conversions_metric` says "Whether this conversion action should be included in the "conversions" metric". An action with `primary_for_goal` false "is non-biddable for all campaigns regardless of their customer conversion goal or campaign conversion goal", but "custom conversion goals do not respect primary_for_goal". Each action has `attribution_model_settings.attribution_model`, `click_through_lookback_window_days` and `view_through_lookback_window_days`.
    * The guide [Conversion goals](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/conversions/goals/overview) of the Google Ads API, last updated on 13 November 2026. Campaign goals override the goals of the customer. For an action with `primary_for_goal` true, "Google Ads includes the conversion action's conversions and conversion values in Conversions and All conversions". For one with it false, Google "doesn't include the conversion action in bidding or Conversions unless you use the conversion action in a CustomConversionGoal", and its values stay in All conversions. "The include_in_conversions_metric attribute of ConversionAction is deprecated", and the guide asks to move reporting logic that relies on it to `primary_for_goal`.
    * The [insights of an ad account](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/reference/ad-account/insights) of the Meta Marketing API, whose examples call v25.0 and which states no version; the connector calls v27.0, and `meta_ads.py` says that no field that the macros of `meta_v26.sql` read changed in v27.0. `action_values` is a `list<AdsActionStats>`, "The total value of all conversions attributed to your ads", and `actions` "The total number of actions Accounts Center accounts took that are attributed to your ads". `purchase_roas` and `website_purchase_roas` are lists of ratios, "The total return on ad spend (ROAS) from purchases" and "from website purchases".
