# The data model

The warehouse holds the advertising statistics of every platform in one model, by day at the grains of a campaign, an ad group and an ad, with the clients of the business, the conversions of each conversion action, the sessions of web analytics and the exchange rates into one reporting currency ([ADR-0024](../adr/0024-one-deployment-is-one-business.md), [ADR-0025](../adr/0025-model-advertising-statistics-once.md), [ADR-0034](../adr/0034-define-a-basic-data-set-and-the-modules-of-an-extended-one.md)). Metabase reads its marts only. Its data comes from the demo, a fictional business on four fictional platforms in the shapes of real APIs with a fictional source of exchange rates, and from the accounts of Google Ads and the rates of the ECB once a deployment connects them ([Google Ads](#google-ads), [The rates of the ECB](#the-rates-of-the-ecb)). The connectors of the other real platforms are planned.

!!! note "Planned for v0.5"

    [ADR-0035](../adr/0035-keep-four-layers-and-erase-the-raw-layer-after-acceptance.md) to [ADR-0037](../adr/0037-serve-the-marts-as-views-over-the-core.md) decide to keep the data in a new schema `core` and to turn the marts into views under the same names. The schema `core` exists, with its tables ([The schema core](#the-schema-core)): its journal of loads, `core.loads`, holds the loads that each run of the transform pins and reads ([The raw layer](#the-raw-layer)), and its registries, dimensions and versions of the entities hold what each run reads ([Dimensions and versions](#dimensions-and-versions)), while its facts wait to be filled. The marts read none of it, and this page describes the model as it is today.

## Layers

| Schema | What it holds | Written by | Read by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `raw_<platform>` | The responses of each platform as dlt loads them with `COPY`: nested objects become columns joined by `__`, lists become child tables, and an empty string becomes NULL; the rows of each load of a fact table under its own `_dlt_load_id`, with the journal of the loads ([The raw layer](#the-raw-layer)) | `loader` | `transformer` |
| `raw_<platform>_staging` | The versions of the entities of a load on their way into `raw_<platform>`, which dlt empties after each load | `loader` | nobody else |
| `raw_settings` | The settings of the model of the deployment and the clients of the demo, loaded at the start of each run of the transform | `loader` | `transformer` |
| `staging` | A view for each table of each platform and of the settings, in the names of the model (`stg_<platform>__<table>`, `stg_settings__<table>`); the table of the newest pinned load of each account and day, `stg_loads__newest_days`, which each run of the transform fills from `core.loads` ([The raw layer](#the-raw-layer)); the steps that the platforms share (`int_<entity>__<verb>`), views as well, but for the statistics of campaigns, of ad groups and of ads and the rates of each day (`int_fx_rates__filled`), which are tables, so that each of their tests and each model that reads them reads a table rather than computes them again, among them the views over which the core takes its dimensions and versions (`int_<entity>__current`, [Dimensions and versions](#dimensions-and-versions)); and the seeds of the base package (`seed_…`) | `transformer` | nobody else |
| `marts` | The tables of the model, rebuilt in full on each run of the transform | `transformer` | `metabase_reader` |
| `ops` | The tables in which elementary records each run of dbt and its tests, and the four views of the Ops collection over them ([The schema ops](#the-schema-ops)) | `transformer` | `metabase_reader`, the four views only |
| `core` | The facts, dimensions, registries, journals and rates that are to keep the data for good from v0.5 on, the versions of the campaigns, ad groups and ads, and the table of its migrations; the journal of loads holds the loads that the runs of the transform pinned, and the dimensions, the registries and the versions what the runs read, while the facts, the other journals and the rates hold no row yet ([The schema core](#the-schema-core)) | `migrator` creates the tables, through the migrations that `db-init` applies, and `transformer` the versions, which are its own; `transformer` writes the rows of the journal of loads, of the dimensions, the registries and the versions, and is to write those of the others | `transformer`; `loader`, the journal of loads only |

The staging views of a platform are calls of one macro for each shape of an API (`dbt/macros/shapes/`), so that the demo platform `demo_search` and the connector of Google Ads share one. A macro of statistics or conversions takes the level of its report: a campaign, an ad group or an ad, and reads the rows of the newest load of each account and day that the run of the transform pinned ([The raw layer](#the-raw-layer)). dbt replaces a view or a table by dropping the old one with every view that depends on it, so build nothing of your own on a relation of `staging` or `marts`: put a question of your own in Metabase, whose SQL PostgreSQL does not track. Between two tasks of one run, and after a run that a test stopped, a view of `staging` can be missing; the marts keep their data.

## The raw layer

The raw layer is temporary: it is to hold only the loads that the core has not accepted, and a step after each run of the transform is to delete the rows of the loads that the core has accepted ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-keep-four-layers-and-erase-the-raw-layer-after-acceptance.md)). The core accepts no load yet, and nothing deletes them, so each load adds its window to `raw_<platform>` until then: the demo adds the window of each platform every day.

- **A load appends.** dlt appends the rows of each fact table to `raw_<source>` under the `_dlt_load_id` of the load, with no keys and no merge, through `COPY` ([ADR-0038](../adr/0038-load-by-copy-and-write-the-core-by-whole-days.md)). A load of a platform of the demo, of the rates of the ECB or of Google Ads writes its tables and its journal in one run of dlt, so under one id. The entities keep the shape that they had: dlt keeps the versions of the campaigns, ad groups, ads and conversion actions with its strategy `scd2`, through `raw_<source>_staging`, as `ad_campaign_names` reads the history of the names, and replaces the accounts and the properties whole. A new table or column of a platform reaches raw, and a value of another type than its column stops the load before it writes anything (the contract of the schema of dlt, with the types frozen). dlt makes a new table with no index on `_dlt_id`.
- **The journal of the loads.** Each load also appends a row for each account to `raw_<source>._agoranomos_loads`, in the same run as its rows:

    | Column | What it holds |
    |---|---|
    | `account_id` | The account as its platform names it: the ID of a property for `demo_web`, and `EUR`, the base of the rates, for `demo_fx` and `ecb` |
    | `status` | `loaded`, or `failed` for an account of Google Ads whose request to the API failed |
    | `window_from`, `window_to` | The first and the last day of the window that the load took of the account, in its time zone; none for a failed account |
    | `as_of` | The day of the load in the time zone of the account; none for a failed account |
    | `now` | The moment of the run of the DAG, which a retry keeps |
    | `loaded_at` | The moment of the load; none for a failed account |
    | `error` | The error of the API of a failed account, which names no secret |
    | `_dlt_load_id` | The load, as in the rows of its facts |

- **A whole load.** dlt writes the row of a load into `raw_<source>._dlt_loads`, with `status = 0`, after the last table of the load. A load that stopped half way has none, its rows are read by nobody, and the next load takes its days again, as a load starts its window after the last day of a whole load.
- **The pin.** The first task of dbt of each run of the transform, `pin`, after the settings and before every other task of dbt, runs `dbt run-operation pin_loads` (`dbt/macros/pin_loads.sql`) as `transformer` ([ADR-0046](../adr/0046-pin-the-loads-before-the-transform-and-accept-them-before-the-cleanup.md)). In one transaction, it copies into `core.loads` the rows of the journal of every whole load of each source that `core.loads` does not hold yet, one for each load and account: its window, or its error for a failed account, the moment when the load ended, from `_dlt_loads`, the ID of the call of dbt that pinned it, and the status `pinned`. A source that no load has written is passed over. Then it creates the partitions of the facts of the core for every month of the pinned windows, through `core.ensure_partition`, each in a transaction of its own and outside any model ([Partitions](#partitions)): the four facts of ads for an ad platform, that of sessions for web analytics, and none for the rates. The run reads the loads of `core.loads` and no other, so a load that ends while the run runs, between two of its tasks of dbt, waits for the next run, however many calls of dbt the run makes; a pin in the hook `on-run-start` of dbt would pin again in each of them, one for each node. Only a run of the DAG pins: the macro refuses to run without the variable `DBT_JOB_RUN_ID`, which the DAG gives it, so dbt by hand, such as `dbt build --select tag:critical`, reads what the runs pinned and pins nothing. A pin that fails stops the run before any other task of dbt, and the next run pins what it had not. Nothing accepts a load yet, so every row stays `pinned`, and `core.loads` grows by the accounts of each load.
- **What staging reads.** For each account and day, the staging model of a fact table reads the rows of the newest load that the runs pinned whose window covers the day (`dbt/macros/newest_load.sql`): of the loads of the source in `core.loads` with a window and the status `pinned` or `accepted`, the one with the largest `load_id`, compared as a number, as it is a time in seconds with a fraction of varying length, when it is a pinned one, as the core is to hold the days of an accepted load. So an older load that ends after a newer one was accepted does not replace its days. Of the accepted loads, only those newer than the oldest pinned load of the account count, as only such a load can win a day. The table `staging.stg_loads__newest_days` holds that load for each source, account and day, with a key of the four in one column, which each staging model of a fact compares with the same of each row: each run of the transform fills the table once from `core.loads`, before the staging models of the facts, and analyzes it, so that PostgreSQL plans the join from its statistics, and as `core.loads` changes only at the pin, a build of the table by hand gives the same rows. A view in its place made each staging model of a fact a subquery that the planner took for one row, repeated in each model above it. So a load replaces the days of its window whole: a row that a platform no longer sends is not read, and a day that the newest load covers and does not hold is empty. A day that only an older load covers keeps it, as after a short window, and a failed account keeps the load before. The child tables of the actions of `demo_social` follow their rows by `_dlt_parent_id`.
- **What staging depends on.** A load is pinned only while `_dlt_loads` holds its row and the journal its rows, so neither may lose the rows of a load that no run has pinned; a pinned load is read while `core.loads` holds its rows and raw its facts. No test of dbt reads a raw table: the checks of the data run on the staging views ([ADR-0046](../adr/0046-pin-the-loads-before-the-transform-and-accept-them-before-the-cleanup.md)).

## The marts

| Mart | One row is | Key |
|---|---|---|
| `ad_stats_campaign_daily` | A campaign on a day: impressions, clicks, spend, and the conversions of its primary conversion actions | `report_date`, `platform`, `account_id`, `campaign_id` |
| `ad_conversions_campaign_daily` | A campaign, a day and a conversion action: conversions and their value | the key above and `conversion_action_id` |
| `ad_stats_ad_group_daily` | An ad group on a day, an ad set of Meta among them: impressions, clicks, spend, and the conversions of its primary conversion actions | `report_date`, `platform`, `account_id`, `ad_group_id` |
| `ad_stats_ad_daily` | An ad on a day: impressions, clicks, spend, and the conversions of its primary conversion actions | `report_date`, `platform`, `account_id`, `ad_group_id`, `ad_id` |
| `web_sessions_daily` | A property of web analytics on a day, by source, medium and campaign: sessions, engaged sessions, key events and revenue | `report_date`, `property_id`, `session_source`, `session_medium`, `session_campaign_name`, `session_campaign_id` |
| `ad_platforms` | A platform: its name, its kind, and for a demo platform the API that it mimics | `platform` |
| `ad_accounts` | An ad account: its client, currency, time zone and status, and the first and last day of its statistics | `platform`, `account_id` |
| `clients` | A client of the business: a brand, shop or legal entity | `client_id` |
| `ad_campaigns` | A campaign: its current name, status and type, the days of its statistics, and how many names it had | `platform`, `account_id`, `campaign_id` |
| `ad_campaign_names` | A name that a campaign had, from when and until when | `platform`, `account_id`, `campaign_id`, `valid_from` |
| `ad_groups` | An ad group: its campaign, its current name, status and type, and the days of its statistics | `platform`, `account_id`, `ad_group_id` |
| `ads` | An ad: its campaign and ad group, its current name, status and type, and the days of its statistics | `platform`, `account_id`, `ad_group_id`, `ad_id` |
| `fx_rates_daily` | A currency on a day: its rate into the reporting currency, `rate_into_reporting_currency` | `rate_date`, `currency`, `reporting_currency` |

The marts store no ratio, as a sum of ratios is wrong: CTR, CPC, CPM, cost per conversion, conversion rate and ROAS are metrics of Metabase over `ad_stats_campaign_daily`, `ad_stats_ad_group_daily` and `ad_stats_ad_daily`, in the collection agoranomos / Metrics, which the marts declare in dbt, each named with its level, as `CTR (Campaigns)`, `CTR (Ad Groups)` and `CTR (Ads)` ([Describe the marts](describe-the-marts.md#metrics)). Every column has a description in the database and in Metabase. The facts carry the names of their platform, client, account and campaign, and of their ad group and ad, so that a question needs no join. Each level comes from a report of its own of the platform, never as a sum of a lower one, so a sum of the ad groups of a campaign is not its row: a Performance Max campaign of Google has no ad groups and no ads, and its statistics are in `ad_stats_campaign_daily` only. An ad of a type that has no name on its platform, such as a responsive search ad of Google, has its ID as its name. The marts have primary keys and no foreign keys in PostgreSQL, and tests check the relationships. In Metabase, `client_id` and `platform` are foreign keys of `clients` and `ad_platforms`, the two marts whose key is one column ([Describe the marts](describe-the-marts.md#foreign-keys)).

## Clients

A deployment is one business, which groups its ad accounts into clients ([ADR-0024](../adr/0024-one-deployment-is-one-business.md)). Name them in `extensions/model/clients.toml`:

```toml
[clients.acme]
name = "Acme Outdoor"

[[accounts]]
platform = "google_ads"
account_id = "1234567890"
client = "acme"
```

A client ID is lower-case letters, digits, `-` and `_`, at most 64 characters, and starts with a letter or a digit; `unassigned` is reserved. The client of an account is one that the same file names in `[clients]`. An account ID is written as the API of its platform gives it, such as `1234567890` for a customer of Google Ads, without the dashes that its UI shows. An account of a real platform counts once its connector loads it: today that is Google Ads, and the other platforms are planned.

An account that no file names belongs to the client `unassigned`, named Unassigned, and a test warns about it. The first task of each run of the transform reads the file and checks it before anything is written; a mistake fails that task with one line that names the file and the entry, and the marts keep their data. A change takes effect at the next run of the transform: trigger `agoranomos_demo_transform` in Airflow to see it now. The clients of the demo come from a file of the same form in the base package and show in `clients` only while the data has accounts or properties of the demo; a client or an account that both name is the deployment's. [Configuration](configuration.md#the-settings-of-the-model) lists the limits of the files.

## Conversions

Each platform reports conversions by conversion action: an action of Google, an action type of Meta, a metric of TikTok. `ad_conversions_campaign_daily` has every action, with a category (`purchase`, `lead`, `sign_up`, `add_to_cart`, `app_install` or `other`) and whether it is primary. The `conversions` and `conversion_value` of `ad_stats_campaign_daily` are the sums of its primary actions, and those of `ad_stats_ad_group_daily` and `ad_stats_ad_daily` the sums of the primary actions of the report by action of their own level; the conversions by action are kept at the grain of campaigns only ([ADR-0034](../adr/0034-define-a-basic-data-set-and-the-modules-of-an-extended-one.md)). For Google Ads the account says which actions are primary: an action counts when Google counts it in its own metric of conversions, by the flag `include_in_conversions_metric` of the action ([Google Ads](#google-ads)). For the platforms of the demo the seed `seed_conversion_actions` of the base package says it. A deployment overrides either, or sets an action of its data that neither names, in `extensions/model/conversion_actions.toml`:

```toml
[[actions]]
platform = "demo_social"
conversion_action_id = "offsite_conversion.fb_pixel_lead"
is_primary = false
conversion_category = "lead"
```

`platform`, `conversion_action_id` and `is_primary` are required, and `conversion_category` is optional: an entry without it keeps the category of the seed, or the one that its platform reports. An action that none of them names is not primary, and its category is the one that its platform reports, or `other`. Meta counts a purchase both by the pixel and in `omni_purchase`: in the seed the pixel's is primary, and only one of them should be, or a purchase counts twice.

## Currencies

Money is `numeric(18,6)`, and no amount is rounded to cents in the marts. In the raw tables it is text, as whole micros or in units, so that a load keeps every digit that the platform gave: staging casts it, and the type `decimal` of dlt, which loses the tenth decimal place without a word, reaches no column ([ADR-0038](../adr/0038-load-by-copy-and-write-the-core-by-whole-days.md)). A fact has its money in the currency of its account (`spend`, `conversion_value`, beside `currency`) and in the reporting currency (`spend_reporting`, `conversion_value_reporting`, beside `reporting_currency`); the sessions have `total_revenue` and `total_revenue_reporting` in the same way. An amount in the reporting currency is the amount of the account times the rate of its day, to six places. Add up money across accounts only in the columns that end in `_reporting`: Metabase shows them as money, and the others as plain numbers. The reporting currency is the setting `AGORANOMOS_REPORTING_CURRENCY` ([Configuration](configuration.md)): `EUR` or a currency of the reference rates of the ECB, and `USD` or `EUR` with the rates of the demo alone.

The rate of a day is the reference rate of the euro published on that day, or the last one before it on a day without a publication, such as a weekend; `fx_rates_daily.is_carried_forward` says which. A rate between two currencies goes through the euro: one unit of a currency is worth `rate(EUR → reporting) / rate(EUR → currency)` units of the reporting currency, to eight places. The reporting currency has the rate 1 on every day, and another currency has a rate from the first day that the source published one. The rates of the demo are fictional. The source `ecb` loads the reference rates of the European Central Bank, 29 currencies as of October 2026, when the DAG `agoranomos_ecb_daily` is unpaused ([The rates of the ECB](#the-rates-of-the-ecb)). Once it has rates, the model takes them alone, and those of the demo serve only a deployment without them ([ADR-0032](../adr/0032-connect-google-ads-first-with-the-reference-rates-of-the-ecb.md)).

## Days and time zones

`report_date` is the day in the time zone of the account (`ad_accounts.time_zone`), as the platforms report it: a daily total cannot be moved into another zone. A sum over accounts in different time zones adds up days that cover different hours, and the last day with data can differ by account. In the `Etc` zones of the tz database the sign is that of POSIX: `Etc/GMT+8` is eight hours behind UTC.

## Names of campaigns

Every row of a campaign carries its current name, so that a group by name does not split a renamed campaign. `ad_campaign_names` keeps the names that each campaign had, as the loads saw them; `web_sessions_daily.session_campaign_name` keeps the name that the links carried on the day. Link a session to its ad campaign by the campaign ID in `utm_id`: `web_sessions_daily.ad_platform` and `ad_account_id` name the campaign whose ID the links carried, when exactly one campaign of the data has it.

A name that a platform leaves empty is no name. A load writes it as NULL, and staging reads an empty name of an earlier load as NULL too (the macro `to_name`), so the two count alike: an ad without a name takes its ID, and a campaign or an ad group without one fails its test, as every platform requires a name there.

## Quality

The tests of dbt run right after their model, in the same task, `dbt build` ([ADR-0028](../adr/0028-build-each-node-of-dbt-with-its-tests-in-one-task.md)). Every test with the severity `error`, the default, carries the tag `critical` ([ADR-0038](../adr/0038-load-by-copy-and-write-the-core-by-whole-days.md)), and `dbt build --select tag:critical` runs them by hand, as `CONTRIBUTING.md` describes. Those in `staging`, on the staging views and on the steps that the platforms share, guard the marts: a failure stops the models that read the model, and the marts keep their data. They check that the day, the platform and the account of each row of a fact are there, that the lists of the entities have unique keys and names, that every day and currency of the facts has a rate, that every account and campaign of the facts is known, and that the primary actions add up to the conversions of each campaign. What one account can get wrong alone, the checks of the quarantine check for each account instead, so that the others go on ([The quarantine](#the-quarantine)). Warnings do not stop anything: a day missing in the data of an account, more clicks than impressions, an account without a client, ad groups that add up to more than their campaign on a day, or ads to more than their ad group, beyond a cent for each row, as two reports loaded at different moments can disagree on recent days, an account in quarantine, and a source that stopped. The package elementary records the status of every test of every run, and the number of rows that it found, in the schema `ops`, which the Ops collection shows ([Watch the pipeline](../how-to/watch-the-pipeline.md)).

## The schema ops

The schema `ops` holds the tables of the package elementary, in which its hooks record each run of dbt, each model, seed and test of it, and the models and tests of the project, and the views of the project that the Ops collection reads ([ADR-0029](../adr/0029-record-the-runs-of-dbt-with-elementary-and-show-them-in-ops.md)). A test records its status and the number of rows that it found, never the rows. `metabase_reader` reads the five views and no table of elementary, which also keep the SQL of the models and the settings of each run.

| View | One row for | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `ops_loads` | Each account of each platform | The last day that a load took for the account, when it was loaded, how many hours ago, and how many loads the journal holds, from `_agoranomos_loads` of each schema `raw_<platform>` |
| `ops_quarantine` | Each account that the last run of the transform kept out of the core, and each reason | What the checks found, how many loads they kept out, the days that the core did not take, and since when the journal `core.account_quarantine` holds the reason for a load of the account that is still pinned ([The quarantine](#the-quarantine)) |
| `ops_transform_nodes` | Each model, seed, snapshot and test in each run of dbt | Its status, its seconds, when it started and ended, the rows that it changed or found, what dbt said of it, and the DAG and the run of Airflow whose task ran dbt |
| `ops_transform_tests` | Each test in each run of dbt | The model and the column that it checks, its kind, its severity, its status and the rows that it found |
| `ops_warehouse_tables` | Each table of the warehouse | The rows that the last ANALYZE estimated and the bytes that the table takes with its indexes, when the view is read |

A task of the transform runs dbt for one node, so one run of the transform is many runs of dbt, which share `airflow_run_id`. dbt rebuilds the views on each run; elementary keeps the rows of its tables, and nothing removes old rows yet.

## The schema core

!!! note "Filled in v0.5"

    The schema `core` is to keep the data for good: narrow facts by grain, dimensions with their current attributes, the registries of accounts and of conversion actions, the rates by day, and the journals of the loads and of the restatements ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-keep-four-layers-and-erase-the-raw-layer-after-acceptance.md), [ADR-0036](../adr/0036-model-the-core-as-narrow-facts-and-versioned-dimensions.md), [ADR-0038](../adr/0038-load-by-copy-and-write-the-core-by-whole-days.md)). Its tables exist: the journal of loads holds the rows that the pin of each run of the transform writes ([The raw layer](#the-raw-layer)), the registries, the dimensions and the versions of the entities those that dbt merges in each run ([Dimensions and versions](#dimensions-and-versions)), and the journal of quarantine those that the checks of each run find ([The quarantine](#the-quarantine)); dbt is to write the facts, the other journals and the rates in v0.5, and until then the marts are built as before and read none of them.

The role `migrator` owns the schema and every table, sequence and function that a migration creates in it. It cannot sign in and has no password: `db-init` applies each migration as the superuser of the warehouse with `SET ROLE migrator`, so `transformer`, which is to write into the tables of the core, can drop, empty or change none of them, nor the schema, which only their owner can ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-keep-four-layers-and-erase-the-raw-layer-after-acceptance.md)). Each table and column of the core is described in the migration that creates it, as dbt describes only what it owns (`persist_docs` needs ownership), and SQL clients read the descriptions in the warehouse.

| Table | One row is | Partitions | `transformer` may |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ad_stats_campaign_daily` | A campaign of an account on a day: impressions, clicks, spend, the conversions of the metric of the platform and all conversions, with their values, in the currency of the account | By month of `report_date` | Read, insert and delete rows |
| `ad_stats_ad_group_daily` | An ad group of an account on a day, with its campaign and the measures above | By month of `report_date` | Read, insert and delete rows |
| `ad_stats_ad_daily` | An ad of an ad group of an account on a day, with its campaign and the measures above | By month of `report_date` | Read, insert and delete rows |
| `ad_conversions_campaign_daily` | A conversion action of a campaign of an account on a day: its conversions and their value | By month of `report_date` | Read, insert and delete rows |
| `web_sessions_daily` | A property of web analytics on a day, by source, medium and campaign: sessions, engaged sessions, key events and revenue | By month of `report_date` | Read, insert and delete rows |
| `dim_account` | An account of an ad platform or a property of web analytics, with its surrogate key `account_sk`, its client, currency, time zone and status, whether the settings still name it, and the first and last day of its statistics | None | Read and change rows |
| `dim_conversion_action` | A conversion action of a platform, with its surrogate key `conversion_action_sk`, its category, and whether it is primary | None | Read and change rows |
| `dim_client` | A client of the business | None | Read and change rows |
| `dim_campaign`, `dim_ad_group`, `dim_ad` | A campaign, an ad group or an ad, with its current name, status, common state (`active`, `paused`, `removed` or `other`), type, the days of its statistics, and whether the platform still lists it | None | Read and change rows |
| `dim_date` | A day of the calendar, with its year, quarter, month and week | None | Read and change rows |
| `dim_campaign_versions`, `dim_ad_group_versions`, `dim_ad_versions` | A version of a campaign, an ad group or an ad: its name, status, common state, type, dates and budget, from when and until when it held ([Dimensions and versions](#dimensions-and-versions)) | None | Own it: dbt creates it, not a migration |
| `fx_rates_daily` | A currency on a calendar day: its rate into a reporting currency through the base currency of the source, with the rate as the source published it and whether it is carried from an earlier day | None | Read and change rows |
| `loads` | A load of a source for an account: its window or its error, and whether a run of the transform has pinned or accepted it | None | Read and change rows; `loader` may read it |
| `account_quarantine` | A load, an account and the check that kept its window out of the core | None | Read and change rows |
| `platform_totals_daily` | The totals of an account on a day as the platform gives them, against which its campaigns are checked | None | Read and change rows |
| `ad_stats_campaign_daily_restatements`, `ad_stats_ad_group_daily_restatements`, `ad_stats_ad_daily_restatements` | A row of a fact of statistics that a load changed or removed, with its old values, the load that had written it and the one that replaced it | By month of `replaced_at`, kept 13 months | Read and change rows |
| `schema_migrations` | A migration of the core that `db-init` has applied | None | Nothing |

A fact carries the surrogate of its account, the IDs of the platform as `bigint` and its amounts as `numeric(18,6)` with the currency in every row; its key ends in the day, and an index on the account and the day serves a filter on an account and a period. Its counters and amounts are zero or more, but for the revenue of web analytics, which refunds can make negative. Its one foreign key points at `dim_account`; the journals have none. A dimension of an entity holds its ID both as `bigint`, which the facts carry, and as text, which the marts are to show, as the IDs of Meta exceed what a browser keeps exact. `transformer` may also take the next value of the sequences of `dim_account` and `dim_conversion_action`, which give their surrogate keys. `loader` may enter the schema to read `loads`, from which a load is to start the window of an account after its last accepted day. `metabase_reader` and `ai_reader` get no right on the core, as the views of v0.5 are to read it with the rights of their owner.

### Dimensions and versions

Each run of the transform writes the registries and the dimensions of the core, and the versions of the campaigns, ad groups and ads, from what it reads: the loads that it pinned and the lists of the entities in raw ([ADR-0036](../adr/0036-model-the-core-as-narrow-facts-and-versioned-dimensions.md), [ADR-0038](../adr/0038-load-by-copy-and-write-the-core-by-whole-days.md)). No mart reads them yet: the marts are built from staging as before.

- **The views under them.** `int_accounts__current`, `int_campaigns__current`, `int_ad_groups__current` and `int_ads__current`, views of `staging`, give one row for each account and entity whose data the run takes into the core, with its current attributes: those of the newest version that a list of its account gave, whether the newest list still names it or not. An account, a campaign, an ad group or an ad that only the data names, and no list, is a stub: an account takes its name, currency and time zone from no list, and an entity takes the name of its newest row of statistics, so that each row of a fact has its dimension. The views of the entities take their accounts from `int_accounts__current`, and the registry of accounts its rows, so an account in quarantine in a run ([The quarantine](#the-quarantine)) is left out there once for all of them. So a stub of an account never enters: the checks hold an account that no list gives out of the core.
- **The merge.** Each dimension and registry is an incremental model of dbt (`dbt/models/core/`) that merges into the table of its migration, with `full_refresh: false`, `on_schema_change: fail` and no `persist_docs`: dbt can neither rebuild the table, which would drop the views over it, nor change its columns, which only its owner may, and a model whose columns or types no longer match its table fails. The migrations describe the tables, and the YAML of each model says the same. A model merges only the rows that are new or that changed, so a run that pins no new load writes no row.
- **The keys.** A new account or conversion action takes the next value of the sequence of its registry and keeps it for good: dbt writes every column of a table that it merges into, so the model takes the key as the one that the table holds, or `nextval`, and the merge leaves it out of what it updates. A campaign, an ad group and an ad have the key of their account and their ID as `bigint`, and their ID as text too; an ID that is not a whole number stops the model.
- **What a row holds.** `is_listed` says whether the newest list of its account names the entity, and `last_seen_at` when a list of its account last named it: for an entity that the newest list names, the end of the newest load of its account that the runs pinned; for one that the platform no longer lists, the moment that the dimension held, or else the start of its newest version; none for a stub. It only moves forward. `first_report_date` and `last_report_date` are those of the statistics that the run reads, and only widen, as the run reads only the loads that the core has not accepted. `name_count` counts the names of the versions of the campaign in the lists of raw and in `dim_campaign_versions`. Every run sets the client of each account and `is_active`, whether the settings of the model name it, from the settings as they are, also for an account that the run does not read; an account that leaves the settings keeps its row. No connector gives the dates or the budget of a campaign or an ad group yet, so they are none, and every platform of the model has the three levels of the core, so `hierarchy_rule` is none.
- **The common state.** `campaign_state`, `ad_group_state` and `ad_state` map the status of each platform, through one macro of its shape in `dbt/macros/shapes/`, which the staging views of the entities call; a status that the macro does not name is `other`, as is a stub, which has no status.

    | Shape | `active` | `paused` | `removed` |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Google Ads, `demo_search` | `ENABLED` | `PAUSED` | `REMOVED` |
    | Meta, `demo_social` | `ACTIVE` | `PAUSED` | `DELETED`, `ARCHIVED` |
    | TikTok, `demo_video` | `ENABLE` | `DISABLE` | `DELETE` |

- **The versions.** `dim_campaign_versions`, `dim_ad_group_versions` and `dim_ad_versions` are snapshots of dbt (`dbt/snapshots/`) over the views above, with the strategy `check` on the name, the status, the type, the dates and the budget of a campaign or an ad group, and on the name, the status and the type of an ad. dbt creates them in `core` as `transformer`, which may create tables there and owns them, so `persist_docs` describes them. A version holds from `dbt_valid_from` until `dbt_valid_to`, the moments of the runs of the snapshot that saw it start and end, in UTC, and the version that holds ends on 31 December 9999 (`dbt_valid_to_current`), not at none. An entity that a run does not read keeps its version (`hard_deletes: ignore`), as a run does not read every account. The versions start at the first run that builds them: the versions that the lists of raw keep from earlier loads, such as those that the history of the demo replays, are not in them. A snapshot reads a view of `staging` and never a table of raw, which may not exist yet: on a warehouse with no load, `dbt build` creates it empty, after the views under it, while `dbt snapshot` alone fails, as those views do not exist yet ([#187](https://github.com/agoranomos/agoranomos/issues/187)). From its second run a snapshot writes through a temporary table, which `transformer` may create.
- **The calendar.** `dim_date` holds every day from 1 January 2000, the first month that a partition of a fact may hold, to the end of the year after the current one, and each run adds the days that it lacks.
- **Tests.** The keys of each dimension and registry, and of each version, are unique; each version ends where the next of its entity begins, and the newest holds; and a warning names each account whose stubs are more than 5% of its entities (`stub_share_warn` in the `vars` of `dbt/dbt_project.yml`), and each platform for the accounts.

### The quarantine

Before it writes the core, each run of the transform checks the data of each account of the ad platforms and of web analytics that it reads, G1 and G1a of [ADR-0035](../adr/0035-keep-four-layers-and-erase-the-raw-layer-after-acceptance.md), and an account that fails a check stays out of that run, while the others go on and the run stays green. The checks are the table `staging.int_g1_failures`, which each run fills anew, with one row for each load, account, reason and kind of data that failed (`dbt/macros/quarantine.sql`); [Watch the pipeline](../how-to/watch-the-pipeline.md#when-an-account-is-in-quarantine) lists the reasons.

- **What they read.** Each kind of facts of staging, the statistics and conversions of the three levels and the sessions, for keys that two rows share or that miss a part, and for counts and amounts that are missing or below zero; the lists of accounts and properties for an account listed twice, a currency outside ISO 4217 and a time zone outside IANA, which a cast to `char(3)` would cut silently; `core.loads` for an account whose newest pinned load failed, and for an account that the pinned loads name and no list gives; the fact tables of raw as they are, of the loads that the run reads, for rows outside the window of their load, which staging never reads, and a spend with more than six decimal places, which staging rounds; and, for G1a, the spend of the campaigns of each load against what `core.ad_stats_campaign_daily` holds on the same days: a load with no row there, `empty_window`, or a fall by more than `quarantine_spend_fall` of the `vars`, 0.5, `spend_fall`.
- **One statement for each kind.** The checks of each kind of data are one statement of their own, an aggregate by the key that counts the duplicates and the values in one pass, and a statement of the checks of the accounts, the raw tables of each source, G1a and the stop; as one statement over the three levels, the checks held a dozen aggregates at once ([#191](https://github.com/agoranomos/agoranomos/issues/191)).
- **The stop of a source.** A source with more than half of the accounts of its pinned loads in quarantine stops: each of its pinned accounts gets the reason `source_stopped`, so none enters the core in that run, and the other sources go on. Exactly half stops nothing. A filter in SQL does it, and not a test at `error`, which would stop the healthy sources too, as one table of the core holds the rows of every source ([#188](https://github.com/agoranomos/agoranomos/issues/188)).
- **Where it applies.** `int_accounts__current`, and so every dimension, every version and the registry of accounts, leaves out each account of `int_g1_failures`, once the model `core.account_quarantine` has recorded it, so the core keeps the data of such an account as it was. The intermediate models of the marts leave it out too, as the marts are still tables of staging, so its rows are missing from the marts for that run.
- **The journal.** `core.account_quarantine` keeps each load, account and reason that a run kept out, with what the checks found, the window that the core did not take, the run of dbt that first found it and when; a run that finds the same load bad again adds no row. A failure of an account that a list gives and no load names has no load and no row in the journal, and stays out all the same.
- **The alarm.** `ops.ops_quarantine` shows the accounts that the last run kept out, the card **Accounts in quarantine** of the Ops collection reads it, and two tests warn: `assert_no_account_is_in_quarantine` and `assert_no_source_is_stopped`.

### Partitions

Each fact and each journal of restatements is partitioned by month of its day, a partition named after its table and its month, such as `ad_stats_campaign_daily_2026_11`, with no DEFAULT partition, so a row of a month without a partition fails with SQLSTATE 23514 and takes its whole statement with it ([ADR-0036](../adr/0036-model-the-core-as-narrow-facts-and-versioned-dimensions.md)). The migrations create the partitions of the month in which they run and of the two after it, in UTC, and the pin of each run of the transform those of the facts for every month of the windows that it pinned ([The raw layer](#the-raw-layer)), so a month of the history of an account has its partitions before a model is to write it. Two functions of `migrator`, which only `transformer` may call, make and drop the others ([ADR-0039](../adr/0039-run-one-dbt-build-in-the-watcher-mode.md), [ADR-0046](../adr/0046-pin-the-loads-before-the-transform-and-accept-them-before-the-cleanup.md)):

* `core.ensure_partition(table, month)` creates the partition of a month of a partitioned table of `migrator`, and returns `false` when it exists. It attaches a table made beforehand, so the table takes SHARE UPDATE EXCLUSIVE and the readers of the views do not wait, and it gives `transformer` MAINTAIN on the partition, for VACUUM. It refuses a table that `migrator` does not own and a month before 2000 or more than two months after the current one, so a wrong date fails rather than making a partition of its own. Of two calls for one month at once, one creates it and the other returns `false`.
* `core.drop_partition(table, month)` drops a month of a journal of restatements, and returns `false` when it has none. It refuses every other table, so no partition of a fact can be dropped through it, as the facts are kept for good ([ADR-0044](../adr/0044-keep-the-core-for-good-and-change-it-by-migrations.md)). It waits at most 2 seconds for its lock.

`transformer` has MAINTAIN on each partitioned table too, which `ANALYZE ONLY` needs: without it, the command skips the table with a WARNING and succeeds.

### The freeze

A row of a fact in a month that ended more than 92 days before the current date, before the window of restatements, is frozen: a trigger on each fact, `freeze_the_months_before_the_window`, refuses its DELETE and its UPDATE, for every role, `migrator` and the superuser too ([ADR-0035](../adr/0035-keep-four-layers-and-erase-the-raw-layer-after-acceptance.md)). The 92 days are the longest window of restatements, the 90 days of Google and Microsoft ([ADR-0038](../adr/0038-load-by-copy-and-write-the-core-by-whole-days.md)), and two days for the time zones of the accounts and of a session. The frozen months move with the date, and every partition takes the trigger of its table. INSERT stays allowed, so the first history of a new account goes in, and a DELETE inside the window costs a comparison for each row and no call of a function.

The setting `agoranomos.thaw` lifts the freeze for one transaction, when it holds the ID of that transaction: `SELECT set_config('agoranomos.thaw', pg_current_xact_id()::text, true)`. A load of history and `data forget` are to set it so. Any role may set a setting of its own prefix, but only the roles that may delete or update the rows of a fact meet the freeze at all; a value that a session set for good, or in an earlier transaction, names another transaction and lifts nothing. `transformer` can neither disable the trigger, which needs the ownership of the table, nor set `session_replication_role`, which turns triggers off and needs the superuser.

### The migrations

| Column of `core.schema_migrations` | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `version` | The number of the migration, the `NNNN` of its file, from 1 |
| `name` | The name of the migration, the part of its file name between the number and `.sql` |
| `checksum` | The SHA-256 of the file as it was applied, in hexadecimal |
| `applied_at` | When the transaction that applied the migration started |

The migrations `0002` to `0007` create the functions of the core, the registries, the dimensions, the facts, the journals and the rates, in that order. [Configuration](configuration.md#the-migrations-of-the-core) says how `db-init` applies the migrations, and [`./agoranomos status`](command-line.md#status) shows those that the warehouse has applied and those that wait. A backup holds the core with its owners, rights, partitions and trigger, and a restore puts them back as they were ([Back up and restore](../how-to/back-up-and-restore.md)).

The demo has a fictional business with two clients, Tarnwick Outfitters and Brindlemoor Haus, and 18 months of history up to the day before its first start, in the time zone of each account, to which a daily load adds each new day ([See the demo](../how-to/see-the-demo.md#keep-the-demo-up-to-date)). The numbers of each campaign of the demo on a day are divided among its ad groups, and those of each ad group among its ads, by shares that vary a little by day, so that they add up exactly, as on a platform. ## Google Ads

The connector of Google Ads, the platform `google_ads`, is the first of a real ad platform ([ADR-0032](../adr/0032-connect-google-ads-first-with-the-reference-rates-of-the-ecb.md), [Connect Google Ads](../how-to/connect-google-ads.md)). The DAG `agoranomos_google_ads_daily` loads, as the role `loader`, the accounts of `google_ads` that `extensions/model/clients.toml` names, from the REST interface of the Google Ads API v26, into `raw_google_ads`: each account (`customers`), its campaigns, ad groups, ads and conversion actions with their versions (`campaigns`, `ad_groups`, `ads`, `conversion_actions`), and the statistics and the conversions by conversion action by day of each of the three levels (`campaign_stats`, `campaign_conversions`, `ad_group_stats`, `ad_group_conversions`, `ad_stats`, `ad_conversions`), each result as the API gives it. The staging models read them through the macros that `demo_search` calls, and `google_ads_customers` for the accounts, which the load reads one by one, so `manager_account_id` stays empty. Each is empty until the first load, as the DAG starts paused. Each load appends the facts of each account under its own id and records its window, or its error, in `_agoranomos_loads` ([The raw layer](#the-raw-layer)). The window of a daily load is the days since the last that a whole load took, and at least the last 90, as on `demo_search`; the first load takes the last 36 months, as Google Ads keeps data by day for 37 only ([ADR-0034](../adr/0034-define-a-basic-data-set-and-the-modules-of-an-extended-one.md)).

A conversion action of Google Ads counts in `ad_stats_campaign_daily`, `ad_stats_ad_group_daily` and `ad_stats_ad_daily` when the account counts it in its conversions: `metrics.conversions` of the API includes only the actions whose `include_in_conversions_metric` is true ([Metrics v26](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/fields/v26/metrics), as of November 2026), and `int_conversion_actions__resolved` takes that flag of the newest version of each action in `conversion_actions` for `is_primary`, unless `extensions/model/conversion_actions.toml` names the action. An action that a manager account owns counts when one of the accounts that list it counts it, and an action that no account lists does not count. `ad_conversions_campaign_daily` has the conversions of every action. The load also keeps `primary_for_goal`, which the model does not read: the guide of conversion goals of Google calls `include_in_conversions_metric` deprecated and names `primary_for_goal` in its place ([Conversion goals](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/conversions/goals/overview), as of November 2026). A campaign with conversion goals of its own counts the primary actions of those goals in Google Ads, so its conversions can differ from the account. Performance Max campaigns have asset groups instead of ad groups, and the API returns none of their ad groups or ads, so they count at the grain of campaigns only.

## The rates of the ECB

The source `ecb` is the first that is not a demo ([ADR-0032](../adr/0032-connect-google-ads-first-with-the-reference-rates-of-the-ecb.md)). The DAG `agoranomos_ecb_daily` loads, as the role `loader`, the daily reference rates of the euro from the data API of the ECB, the series `EXR/D..EUR.SP00.A` as CSV, without a key, into `raw_ecb.rates_daily`, each row as the CSV names its columns, all text. It starts paused, so that the demo alone asks the ECB nothing: unpause it in Airflow to load the rates. It runs every day at 06:47 UTC: its first run takes the rates from the first day of the month 37 months back, one before the history of Google Ads ([ADR-0034](../adr/0034-define-a-basic-data-set-and-the-modules-of-an-extended-one.md)), and each later one the days from the day after the last that a whole load took, or from 7 days back when that is earlier, to yesterday in Frankfurt; each load appends its rates under its own id, and `stg_ecb__rates_daily` reads, for each day, those of the newest whole load ([The raw layer](#the-raw-layer)). The ECB publishes at about 16:00 CET on each working day of TARGET, and asks that it be cited as the source. `stg_ecb__rates_daily` reads the table through the macro `ecb_exr`, and is empty until the first load. Each load updates the asset of the table, and the transform runs after it ([What starts the transform](../how-to/watch-the-pipeline.md#what-starts-the-transform)).

## Upgrading from v0.4

The first `./agoranomos up` of a warehouse of v0.4 with the demo profile finds the raw tables of the demo in the shape of v0.4, whose loads merged the days of each account in place and kept the journal `_agoranomos_reloads`: `demo-init` runs the history again, whose load of each platform drops the tables of `raw_demo_<platform>` and of `raw_demo_<platform>_staging` and loads them anew, each load under its own id, and the transform after it builds the model again ([See the demo](../how-to/see-the-demo.md#what-later-starts-do)). Before the history, `demo-init` asks Airflow to parse the DAG of the transform again: the DAG processor parses an unchanged DAG file every five minutes only, and could otherwise start that run from the manifest of v0.4, without the table `stg_loads__newest_days`. Until that transform ends, the views of `staging` over those tables are gone, and the marts keep their data. The first run of the transform after the update pins every whole load that raw holds by then, and makes the partitions of the facts of the core for their months. The rates of the ECB and the accounts of Google Ads have no journal `_agoranomos_loads` yet, so the next load of each takes its whole history again, the 37 months of the ECB and the 36 of each account of Google Ads, about 230 operations of its daily quota ([Connect Google Ads](../how-to/connect-google-ads.md)); the rows of their earlier loads stay in `raw_ecb` and `raw_google_ads`, which staging does not read, with their journal `_agoranomos_reloads`. Until those loads, staging reads no rate of the ECB and no day of Google Ads, so a run of the transform before them, such as the one after the history of the demo or after the first of the two loads, builds the marts without the data of Google Ads, and with the rates of the demo, if any, in place of those of the ECB. Trigger `agoranomos_ecb_daily` and `agoranomos_google_ads_daily` in Airflow right after the update, so that the marts lack them for one load only.

## Upgrading from v0.1

The first `./agoranomos up` of v0.2.0 removes the demo of v0.1 from the warehouse: the schemas `raw_demo` and `raw_demo_staging`, the view `staging.stg_demo__ad_stats_daily` and the mart `marts.ad_spend_daily`. With the demo profile, it then loads the history of the new demo and builds the model. The collection `Agoranomos demo` moves into the collection agoranomos as Demo, and its dashboard reads the new marts. A question of your own on `marts.ad_spend_daily` stops working: point it at `marts.ad_stats_campaign_daily`, whose `spend_reporting` is the spend in one currency. An `.env` of v0.1 keeps `WAREHOUSE_DB_MEMORY=256m` and `AIRFLOW_TRIGGERER_MEMORY=640m`, which `./agoranomos init` does not change: set them to `384m` and `512m`, the new defaults, before that start ([Update to a new release](../how-to/run-locally.md#update-to-a-new-release)).
